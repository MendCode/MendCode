# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Developers and technical teams who work primarily inside a terminal and use MendCode to coordinate coding agents, review changes, manage durable project memory, and customize their workflow. They need dense information to remain legible through keyboard-driven, mouse-capable terminal interfaces across narrow and wide layouts.

## Product Purpose

MendCode is a customizable coding terminal and agent harness. It makes model routing, review, memory, permissions, packaging, and coordination visible and controllable instead of hiding them behind a chat box. Success means a user can understand state, make informed decisions, and move through complex workflows without leaving the terminal or patching runtime internals.

## Brand Personality

Technical, expressive, and trustworthy. MendCode should feel terminal-native without being austere, customizable without becoming inconsistent, and powerful without obscuring what is real or persisted.

## Anti-references

- Generic coding-agent chat shells that expose no workflow structure.
- OpenCode-clone surfaces that do not establish a distinct MendCode identity.
- Decorative dashboards made from repeated cards and badges.
- Fake visualizations that imply relationships or runtime state not present in persisted data.
- Image-based terminal effects that cannot degrade to readable text.

## Design Principles

1. Show real state. Visual richness must remain grounded in persisted data and honest labels.
2. Let the terminal be spatial. Use the full character grid for navigation, topology, comparison, and focus rather than reducing every workflow to lists.
3. Progressive density. A compact surface should summarize; a dedicated route should support exploration without changing the underlying contract.
4. One capability, one visual language. Core surfaces and package-owned extensions should share reusable primitives instead of drifting into approximations.
5. Customization without fragility. Active themes, terminal capabilities, and layout constraints must be respected with deterministic fallbacks.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

All essential meaning must survive without color, mouse input, animation, or Unicode Braille support. Keyboard navigation, visible focus, readable labels, stable layouts, high-contrast theme roles, reduced-motion behavior, and plain ASCII fallbacks are required. Dense visualizations must expose counts, relation types, selected-node details, and honest truncation or sampling indicators in text.
